Quãng level 4 asks the augmented sixth (A6) among its augmented and diminished
intervals, which is lesson 1's interval. No drill asks augmented sixth chords,
chromatic chords or bass lines, so the other lessons have no practice link.

What each teaches:

1. **Introduction**: a pre-dominant class whose ♭6 and ♯4 approach 5 by half
   step at once; ♭6 up to ♯4 is an augmented sixth that opens out to an octave;
   in minor ♭6 is already in the key signature (shown with a `Cm` signature);
   iv6 with its root raised becomes the augmented sixth. Checks: ♯4 in E minor
   (pad); ♭–♯ interval naming; where the chord leads.
2. **Three types**: It+6 (♭6, ♯4, 1, 1 doubled), Fr+6 (adds 2), Ger+6 (adds ♭3,
   often through i6/4 to avoid parallel fifths, as in Rossini's *William Tell*
   overture per 21.5), the enharmonic German in major (♭3 spelled ♯2, rising to
   the third of I6/4). The book's remark that the names have no known reason is
   a tip. Checks: the French's added note in A minor (pad); naming a German in
   E minor; ♯2 in F major (pad).
3. **Analyzing**: stacked in thirds, all four have a diminished third (F♯–A♭);
   the rule (a diminished third means an augmented sixth, then read the degrees);
   lead-sheet symbols as a Mm7 on ♭6 (Ger = A♭7 with F♯ for G♭, It = A♭7 without
   the fifth, Fr = A♭7♭5), Am–F7–E in A minor; classical spelling shows
   resolution, the symbol shows root and quality. Checks: the d3 interval; a
   French in D minor; the German's symbol in E minor.
4. **Chromatic context**: 21.6's table as two text blocks (quality → secondary
   dominant / secondary diminished; lowered notes → borrowed chord from mode
   mixture; ♭II → Neapolitan; d3 → augmented sixth); 21.8's chromatic
   pre-dominants (iv, iiø7, V/V, vii°/V, N6, It/Fr/Ger) all leading to V; 21.7's
   1–7–♭7–6–♭6–5 bass, alone and harmonized i–V6–i4/2–IV6–Fr+6–V. Checks: a
   V7/V; which chord is not a pre-dominant; the ♭6 in A minor (pad).
5. **Review**: after 21.9 exercise 1 (name the chord: an It+6 in G minor; the
   Ger's lead-sheet symbol in D minor) and exercise 2 (build from a label: ♯4 in
   D minor, the Fr+6 in G major), plus the enharmonic German, the d3 rule,
   a Neapolitan to tell apart (21.6) and the bass line in E minor (21.7).

All examples are our own four-part voicings in C minor/major, A, D, E and G
minor, F and G major. Every augmented sixth resolves to the same V voicing
(`G2 D4 G4 B4` in C) so the half-step motion is easy to hear.

alongside), not in the glossary when written:

Reused from the other chromatic chapters: hợp âm át phụ (secondary dominant),
hợp âm mượn (borrowed chord), pha trộn điệu thức (mode mixture), hợp âm Napoli
(Neapolitan). "Hợp âm giảm phụ" follows the "hợp âm át phụ" pattern; if the
secondary-diminished chapter chose another word, this one should follow it.

Labels: `It+6`, `Fr+6`, `Ger+6`; `EnGer+6` is seven characters, so it is
stacked as `EnGer` over `+6`. Inversions are stacked figures (`V` over `6`,
`i` over `6` over `4`); in prose they are written `i6/4`, `V6`.

## Left out, and why

- **Every song and film theme in 21.5 and 21.7** (Ellington, Coltrane, Mancini,
  Fiona Apple, Menken/Ashman; Harrison, Page/Plant, Tyler, DeLeo/Weiland, Perri):
  copyrighted, so none is named, quoted or linked. The generic progression
  Am–F7–E (i–Ger+6–V) is kept without its source.
- **The classical excerpts of 21.5 and 21.7** (Beethoven's Fifth, Schubert's
  "Der Wegweiser", Rossini's *William Tell*, Schumann's "Am leuchtenden
  Sommermorgen"; Purcell, Bach, Beethoven WoO 80): too long for a phone. Rossini,
  Purcell and Bach are named in tips only; the rest is replaced by our voicings.
- 21.5's "doubly augmented fourth" name for the enharmonic German: one more
  interval name on top of a crowded step.
- 21.7's table comparing eight harmonizations: one harmonization of our own
  instead, with ♭6 on the Fr+6 as in the book's Bach row.
- 21.9's homework and practice test downloads.
- Book order: 21.8 (pre-dominants) is taught before 21.7 (bass lines) in
  lesson 4, so the lesson ends on the bass line and its check.
