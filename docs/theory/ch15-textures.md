Tiết tấu level 3 trains reading and tapping the figures the block-chord and
clave rhythms are made of (dotted quarters, an eighth tied over beat 3).

What each teaches:

1. **Texture**: its vertical side (how many notes or voices sound at once,
   thick and thin) and its horizontal side (rhythmic activity: is the most
   common value slow or fast); chorale texture (a chord for every, or nearly
   every, melody note; melody on top, the lower voices as accompaniment),
   homorhythmic. Checks: count the notes of a five-note chord; which belongs
   to the horizontal side; how the parts of a chorale move.
2. **Arpeggios**: arpeggios over a bass line in octaves; rising, falling, up
   and down; the tenth between bass and top voice; the Alberti bass
   (low–high–middle–high), in any key and register. Checks: an arpeggio's
   direction; a major tenth above {F2} (pad); the third note of an Alberti
   bass on F major (pad).
3. **Block chords**: the "1 (2) &" rhythm; the two-measure "1 2 3 (4) & (1)
   & (2) & 3" rhythm, here in the bass line; repeated eighth-note chords
   (classical, rock, over I–vi–IV–V) and repeated quarter-note chords. Checks:
   where the second chord of "1 (2) &" starts; which rhythm a staff uses; how
   many repeated eighths in 4/4.
4. **Afterbeats and offbeats**: afterbeats (Schoenberg's term: repeated chords
   after the downbeat; sometimes quarters, or figures in thirds); offbeats
   (chords regularly on the upbeats) in the polka and in reggae. Checks: where
   the bass falls; where a polka's chords fall; name an afterbeat pattern.
5. **Clave and bass lines**: the 3–2 son clave and the 2–3; the 3+3+2 rhythm
   in eighths and in sixteenths (tresillo); the habanera as tresillo plus beat
   2; reggaeton built on 3+3+2; the 3+3+3+3+2+2 and 3+3+4+3+3 expansions;
   distinctive bass lines and riffs. Checks: 3–2 or 2–3; where 3+3+2's second
   attack falls; what a riff is.
6. **Review**: vertical side; spot chorale texture; an Alberti bass on G
   (pad); a major tenth above {C3} (pad); spot "1 (2) &"; spot polka offbeats;
   spot the 3–2 clave; tresillo or habanera.

All examples are our own: chorale phrases in C and G major, arpeggio, Alberti,
block-chord, afterbeat, polka and reggae patterns in C, F, G and A minor, the
clave family on a bare staff, and an invented bass riff in A minor.

## Left out, and why

- **Every song and score from the book.** 14.1-14.7 illustrate almost entirely
  with pop, rock, film, musical and reggae recordings (and 14.6-14.7 only list
  recordings). They are copyrighted, so none is named, quoted or linked; the
  patterns themselves are generic and are shown in our own examples. Barber's
  Adagio (1936) is not public domain. The public-domain excerpts (Mozart,
  Beethoven, Schubert, Schumann, Handel, Bizet) would need score excerpts with
  a different rhythm in each hand, which a lesson staff cannot draw (see
  below); only names are kept (Mozart's sonatas, Bizet's Carmen, Schoenberg's
  term).
- The book's nickname for the two-measure block-chord rhythm is a pop song's
  title; the lesson calls it by its count, "1 2 3 (4) & (1) & (2) & 3".
- The "1 (2) &" rhythm in diminution, the slower form of the two-measure
  rhythm, and the 8×3 + 4×2 expansion of 3+3+2: one more variant each than a
  phone lesson holds.
- The reggaeton drum pattern (bass and snare) and the clave video: a play
  block sounds one pitched line, no drums.
- Cross rhythms (14.6's intro), the second line drumming pattern, the
  i–VII–VI–VII progression notes, and the history (Bohemia's polka is kept,
  Frankie Yankovic, Bo Diddley, Bob Marley, The Police and UB40 are not).
- 14.7.1 guitar riffs: one clause in the bass-line step.

## Framework gaps hit

- A staff and a play block carry one rhythm stream: a grand-staff chord's
  members share one duration. So a melody cannot hold over a moving
  accompaniment; the patterns are shown alone (the octave bass under the
  arpeggios is written as an eighth, as on the first beat of each group), and
  chorale texture, where all parts move together, is the only full
  melody-and-accompaniment example.
- Play blocks have no drum sounds; clave and 3+3+2 rhythms play on a single
  pitch.
