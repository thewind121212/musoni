No drill asks non-chord tones, so no lesson has a practice link.

Every example is drawn on a grand staff: the chord in the bass staff (root
position, three notes), the melody alone in the treble, and a label under each
non-chord tone: P, N, App, ET, DN, IN, Ant, Ped, Sus, Ret (the book's
abbreviations, the same in both languages; the text introduces each one).
Suspensions carry their figures as stacked labels (`Sus\n4` under the
suspension, `\n3` under the resolution, so the two numbers sit on one line);
the pedal point stacks it under the numeral (`ii\nPed`).

What each teaches:

1. **Passing and neighbor**: a non-chord tone as a note outside the sounding
   chord, named by how it is approached and left (same note, step, leap);
   the passing tone (step, step on in the same direction; two in a row from root
   down to fifth); the upper and lower neighbor; accented (on the beat) and
   unaccented (between beats); chromatic (outside the key), heard as {C#}
   between {C} and {D}. Checks: a passing tone over V; the lower neighbor of
   {C} (pad); is an {F5} on the downbeat accented.
2. **Appoggiatura and escape tone**: the appoggiatura (leap, then step; usually
   accented; either direction, one resolving up); the escape tone (step, then
   leap the other way, usually unaccented); the four patterns side by side on
   one staff. Checks: an appoggiatura over IV; the escape tone above {C5}
   (pad); which is leap-then-step.
3. **Double neighbors, anticipations, pedal points**: the double neighbor on a
   C major arpeggio (like the book's figure 10.6.1, our own notes) and the tip
   to find chord tones first; the incomplete neighbor (10.11, moved up beside
   the double neighbor) as the book's "utility" tone, shown leapt to and left
   by step, unaccented; the anticipation at a V–I cadence, and syncopation
   making anticipations; the pedal point under I–ii–V7–I, no inversion needed
   over a bass pedal, upper and double pedals. Checks: a double neighbor; the
   anticipation of {G5} in {G} major (pad); the bass {C} under ii.
4. **Suspensions and retardations**: the suspension (accented, same note,
   down by step) with preparation, suspension, resolution, tied or struck
   again; its two numbers from the bass, 9–8, 7–6, 4–3, compound intervals
   reduced (11–10 is 4–3); 2–3 (bass suspension), 6–5, and 5–4 / 3–2 over
   inverted chords in a tip; the retardation (same note, up by step), common at
   cadences in Classical slow movements. Checks: the suspended note of a 4–3
   over {G} (pad); a 9–8 by sight; a retardation over IV.
5. **Review**: exercise 1's table as four checks (retardation; escape tone's
   approach and leave; passing tone; appoggiatura's leave), then our own
   examples to analyze: an upper neighbor over V, a 4–3 in {G} major, the
   passing tone between {A4} and {C5} in {F} major (pad, {Bb}), an anticipation
   in {F} major.

All examples are our own, in C, G and F major.

## Left out, and why

- **Every song and score from the book.** The figures quote pop songs, film
  music, a jazz standard and a national anthem, which are copyrighted or need
  a long excerpt, so none is named, quoted or linked; the classical ones
  (Beethoven, Mozart, Haydn, Handel, Bach, Clara Schumann, Kuhlau, Puccini)
  need score excerpts longer than a phone step holds. Each tone is shown in our
  own short example instead.
- 10.1's metrical / sub-metrical / super-metrical qualifiers: three more terms
  for note length against the beat, not needed to name a tone; accented,
  unaccented and chromatic are kept.
- 10.9's decorated suspensions (other notes before the resolution) and figure
  10.9.6's inverted-chord numbers beyond one tip sentence.
- 10.11's incomplete double neighbor: one sentence in the book, no definition.
- 10.12 exercise 2 (analyze two excerpts with lead-sheet symbols and numerals):
  one is a copyrighted song, the other a Schumann song too long for a phone;
  replaced by the review's four short examples.
- Book order: 10.11 (incomplete neighbor) is taught in lesson 3 beside the
  double neighbor, before suspensions, because both are neighbor figures.

## Framework gaps hit

- No independent voices: every event on the grand staff has one duration, so a
  chord cannot be held under a moving melody. The chord is struck again under
  each melody note (a block-chord texture), which also makes each dissonance
  audible.
- A tie on a chord token ties the whole chord and silences the next event in
  playback, so suspensions are shown with the preparation struck again (the
  book allows both); a tied suspension cannot be drawn and heard.
- No drill trains non-chord tones, so no lesson has a practice link.
