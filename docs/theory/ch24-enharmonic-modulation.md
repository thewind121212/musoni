No lesson has a practice link: Hợp âm (`chords`) asks triads only, and nothing
trains seventh chords, augmented sixths or modulation. Every end screen leads
with "Bài tiếp".

What each teaches:

1. **What enharmonic modulation is**: a modulation by pivot chord where the
   pivot is spelled correctly in only one key, so the other spelling has to be
   imagined (the book's "harmonic pun"); the book's own figure idea, C major to
   B minor through G7, voiced by us on a grand staff (I IV G7 | i6/4 V i); G7 as
   V7 in C (root down a 5th to C) and as Ger+6 G B D E♯ in B minor (bass down a
   half step to F♯, dominant function); the two sonorities used: dominant seventh
   and diminished seventh. Checks: where the root of V7 goes (pad C); where the
   Ger+6 bass goes in B minor (pad F♯); why "enharmonic".
2. **V7 and Ger+6 as pivot chords**: usually only the top note is respelled
   (seventh ↔ augmented sixth); the spelling implies the key (dominant seventh on
   5 in major, Ger+6 bass on lowered 6), shown with D♭7 / Ger+6 in F; secondary
   dominants are reinterpreted too: F7 as V7/IV in F major becomes Ger+6 in A minor
   (our own progression); the other direction: Ger+6 in E minor heard as C7, V7 of
   F major (our own progression); as a tip, the less common respelling of the
   bottom three notes (Ger+6 A♭ C E♭ F♯ → G♯7). Checks: the key whose lowered 6 is
   A♭ (pad C); where the Ger+6 bass F goes in A minor (pad E); which tonic C7
   leads to (pad F, ear question with the chord played).
3. **The diminished seventh as pivot chord**: four minor thirds fill the octave,
   so any note can be the root; the four spellings B°7, D°7, E♯°7, G♯°7; each note
   as the leading tone gives four keys (C, E♭, F♯, A minor), drawn with their
   resolutions; a modulation from A minor to C minor through G♯°7 = B°7 (our own);
   lowering one note a half step gives a dominant seventh whose root is that note
   (B°7 → G7; the four: G7, E7, C♯7, B♭7). Checks: the tonic of E°7 (pad F);
   which note was respelled (G♯ → A♭); the root after lowering F in B°7 (pad E).
4. **Review**: E♭7 as V7 (pad A♭), E♭7 respelled as Ger+6 and its key (pad G),
   which note a V7 → Ger+6 respelling changes, a Ger+6 in D minor heard as V7
   (pad E♭), the tonic of F♯°7 (pad G), how many tonics one °7 serves, C♯°7 with
   B♭ lowered (A7). Modelled on 23.4 exercises 1 and 4 (notate, resolve and
   respell a chord) and 2 (Roman numerals to the new key).

## Terms

The glossary words are used as given. Chosen for this chapter, consistent with the
chromatic chapters being written at the same time:

| Tiếng Việt | English |
|---|---|
| chuyển giọng | modulation |
| hợp âm trụ | pivot chord |
| chuyển giọng trùng âm | enharmonic modulation |
| hợp âm sáu tăng Đức (Ger+6) | German augmented sixth |
| hợp âm át phụ | secondary dominant |
| bậc 6 hạ | lowered scale degree 6 (♭6̂) |

"Viết lại trùng âm" (to respell enharmonically) and "chơi chữ" (pun) are used as
plain words, not terms. Inversion figures in text are written i6/4; on the staff
they stack (`'i\n6\n4'`), and the pivot's two meanings stack too (`'V7\nGer+6'`).

## Left out, and why

- 23.1's Shakespeare pun (*Richard III*, "sun of York"): an aside; the lesson keeps
  the idea in one line ("like a pun: one sound with two meanings").
- 23.2's literature examples: Tchaikovsky, *Nutcracker* (1892; public domain, but
  an orchestral excerpt is too long for a phone step), Beethoven, Symphony No. 5,
  II (same reason), and a 1980 musical number (copyrighted, never used). Their
  point (a V7/IV respelled as Ger+6, a Ger+6 spelled as V7 resolving to I) is
  carried by our own progressions in F major → A minor and E minor → F major.
- 23.3's literature examples: Beethoven, *Pathétique* Sonata, I; Symphony No. 5, II
  (vii°4/2 resolving to an unusually spelled Ger+6); Schubert, "Der Atlas". All are
  public domain, but each needs a long score excerpt and inversions of vii°7
  (4/3, 4/2, 6/5) that earlier chapters do not teach. Replaced by our own A minor →
  C minor modulation in root position.
- vii°7 of a secondary chord (vii°7/IV, vii°7/V) and the rule that diminished
  triads are not tonicized: these lean on the tonicization chapter being written
  now, so the lessons stay with vii°7 of the tonic.
- 23.4 exercises 3 and 5 (analyze literature excerpts with audio): need score
  excerpts and recordings; the review asks the same skills on single chords.
- The voicings in the demos follow simple four-part voice leading; we did not
  teach voice-leading rules for the respelled chords (the book does not either).

seventh falling a half step to the root of a dominant seventh) and checked against
standard theory. All notated examples are our own; no music or figures from the
book.
