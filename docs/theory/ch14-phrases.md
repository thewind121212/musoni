What each teaches:

1. **Perfect authentic cadence**: the authentic cadence splits into PAC and
   IAC; a PAC needs V–I, both chords in root position and the tonic (degree 1)
   in the top voice of I; missing any one (top voice not the tonic, an
   inverted chord, vii° in place of V) makes an IAC. Four-part cadences on a
   grand staff, bass split onto the bass staff. Checks: the top note of I in a
   G major PAC (on the pad, black keys shown); V–I with 5 on top (IAC); V with
   B in the bass (IAC, half cadence as a trap).
2. **Sentence**: a phrase whose idea (motive) is repeated or sequenced, then
   followed by related or unrelated material to a cadence. Three melodies of
   our own, one per book example: a sequence down a step (the shape of the
   book's first example), a repetition reshaped to fit V with a two-note
   fragment after it (the second), an exact repetition followed by new
   material (the third). Checks: what measure 2 does (sequence); what
   measure 3 takes from the idea; a phrase that is not a sentence.
3. **Period**: conclusive (AC, PC) and inconclusive (HC, DC) cadences, PAC
   more conclusive than IAC; a period's last phrase ends more conclusively
   than its first (HC → IAC/PAC, or IAC → PAC); antecedent and consequent as
   question and answer; parallel (a a′) and contrasting (a b) periods; the
   prime mark; the repeated phrase (a a, tip). The parallel period is the
   opening of Beethoven's "Ode to Joy" (public domain), moved to C major; the
   contrasting one is ours. Checks: which cadence is conclusive; antecedent or
   consequent; parallel or contrasting (by ear, the Ode); a repeated phrase.
4. **Asymmetrical and double periods**: three or five phrases with unequal
   antecedents and consequents (our own a b b); the double period as an
   antecedent group and a consequent group, a b a b′ as the parallel double
   period, the three cadence schemes of the book's summary; the repeated
   period, told apart by the cadence after phrase 2. Cadence schemes are
   drawn as their cadences on a grand staff with a "Nghe" button. Checks:
   counting antecedents and consequents; which scheme fits a double period;
   HC–PAC–HC–PAC is a repeated period.
5. **Phrase groups, chains and elisions**: neither is a period, both end with
   a HC; a group's phrases begin alike, a chain's contrast (a b, a b c); the
   elision, where the cadence measure of one phrase is the first measure of
   the next (the shared note highlighted, labelled `PAC` over `2`). The
   summary table (13.8) is the recap. Checks: why a (IAC) a′ (HC) is not a
   period; a (PAC) b (HC) is a chain; what the last measure of phrase 1 is in
   an elision.
6. **Review**: after 13.9: an F major IAC (3 on top), the top note of a D major
   PAC (on the pad), a sentence spotted, the first cadence of a period by ear
   (HC), and the forms from the exercises' answer keys (exercise 1: HC, PAC,
   a b → contrasting period; exercise 2: IAC HC IAC PAC, a b a b′ → parallel
   double period; exercise 3: the prime in a a a a′), plus a phrase group.

Melodies sit on a treble staff in 4/4 with a phrase letter under the first
note and the cadence abbreviation under the last; cadences are four-part
chords on a grand staff. Every idea has a "Nghe" button; melodies play with
a bass note under the cadence where it helps the ear.

## Left out, and why

- **Every quoted excerpt in the chapter** (Mozart K. 331 and K. 333,
  Beethoven Op. 2 No. 1, Op. 10 No. 1, Op. 13, Op. 53, "Für Elise", Haydn,
  Chopin's Prelude Op. 28 No. 20, Couperin, the Beethoven sonatina, Eine
  kleine Nachtmusik, Schumann's "Little Morning Wanderer", and the exercises'
  Mozart, Dédé excerpts): most are public domain, but each needs an 8 to 16
  measure score excerpt, too long for a phone step. Our own short phrases
  carry each idea; only "Ode to Joy" (8 measures of a melody) is kept.
- **"Lean on Me"** (Bill Withers, 1972), the book's pop period: copyrighted,
  left out with no replacement beyond our own examples.
- **The formal diagrams** (figures, images without alt text): written as text
  schemes (a (HC) a′ (PAC)) and as rows of cadences on the staff.
- **Plagal cadence after an inverted IAC** (13.3.1 aside, a root-position PC
  heard as more conclusive than an inverted IAC): a fine point; left out to
  keep the conclusive/inconclusive split clear.
- **a¹ a² a³** in place of many primes: one rare convention, left out.
- **Five-phrase asymmetrical periods**: named in the text, not drawn.
- **Real phrase lengths**: our phrases are 2 or 4 measures; real ones are
  usually 4 or 8. A longer phrase would not fit a phone staff.
- **The homework and practice-test downloads** of 13.9.

## Framework gaps hit

- **A melody with a held bass** cannot be written: a chord token gives every
  member the same duration, so melodies are drawn alone and the play blocks
  add a bass only on the notes it shares a duration with.
- **No drill trains cadences or form**, so no lesson has a practice link.

The cadence abbreviations PAC, IAC, HC, DC, PC are the book's and are used in
both languages (Vietnamese has no common abbreviations); each lesson gives the
Vietnamese name with the abbreviation the first time. "Mô tiến" (glossary:
harmonic sequence) is used for the melodic sequence too.
