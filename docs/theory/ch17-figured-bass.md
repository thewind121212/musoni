Only 17.1 has a practice link: Hợp âm level 4 asks inverted triads by name
and says which inversion each is, which is what the triad figures label. The
drill's Roman mode asks root-position numerals only, so nothing trains
numerals with figures, seventh-chord inversions or six-four types; those end
screens lead with "Bài tiếp".

What each teaches:

1. **Figured bass**: a Baroque shorthand (about 1600-1750): a bass line with
   numbers below for the intervals above the bass; the player voices the chords
   freely, as with lead-sheet symbols, and modern editions realize them; today
   it is taught mainly as inversion symbols. Triad figures 5/3, 6/3, 6/4 and
   their short forms (blank, 6, 6/4); figures count within the key signature;
   a ♯ alone raises the 3rd (from 16.5). Checks: the figure of A C F (6); the
   3rd above a bass E figured ♯ in A minor (G♯, on the pad); the figure of
   D G B (6/4).
2. **Roman numerals with figures**: the figures go after the numeral (I, I6,
   I6/4); chapter 8's interim I/3rd and I/5th become I6 and I6/4; C/E and C/G
   say the same. Checks: D F B in C (vii°6), A D F in C (ii6/4), B D G in G
   (I6); the first two are chords from the book's exercise 1.
3. **Seventh-chord figures**: four positions including third inversion; 7,
   6/5, 4/3, 4/2 and their full forms 7/5/3, 6/5/3, 6/4/3, 6/4/2 (16.5); the
   "7" is dropped once inverted (V6/5, V4/3, V4/2); M, ø and ° stay before the
   figures (IM6/5, viiø4/3). Checks: Fmaj7 over A (6/5); C7 over B♭ in F
   (V4/2); Cmaj7 over G in C (IM4/3, with I4/3 as the trap).
4. **The cadential six-four**: I6/4 just before V in a cadence; it has
   dominant function; the bass holds 5 while 6-5 and 4-3 resolve above;
   formerly called I/5th. Checks: the third chord of a G major phrase
   I–IV–I6/4–V–I; its function; the bass of the cadential six-four in F major
   (C, on the pad).
5. **Other six-four chords**: six-fours occur in only four situations
   (cadential, passing, pedal, melodic bass); passing (bass steps through three
   notes, I–V6/4–I6), pedal (bass repeats, I–IV6/4–I), melodic bass (the bass
   has the melody and reaches the fifth; also called arpeggiated). Checks: a
   passing, a pedal and a melodic-bass six-four to name.
6. **Review**: after exercises 16.6: C/G (I6/4), D°/F in C minor (ii°6),
   A♯°7/G in B minor (vii°4/2), Eø7 in F major (viiø7), Dmaj7/F♯ in D major
   (IM6/5), Em7/G in C (iii6/5), the bass of the cadential six-four in D major
   (A, on the pad), a descending passing six-four in D major.

Every progression is our own stock voicing on a grand staff (bass on the bass
staff, three upper voices on the treble), with the figures as stacked labels
under the chord (`'I\n6\n4'`); seventh-chord inversions are close position on
the bass staff so the intervals are easy to count. In text, a figure is
written with a slash (6/4, V6/5), and lesson 17.1 says the numbers stack on
the staff.

## Left out, and why

- **Handel's Recorder Sonata HWV 362 (16.1)**: public domain, but the figures
  are manuscript and edition images; our own I–I6–IV–V–I bass line shows the
  same idea (bass with figures, then realized).
- **16.3's Mozart K. 331 and "The Star-Spangled Banner"** examples: both are
  public domain but need score excerpts longer than a phone step; our own
  cadences in C and G major carry the cadential six-four.
- **Exercise 4 (write the chords)**: the app has no writing check; its point
  is covered by the identify-the-chord checks.
- **Exercise 5 (Bach Chorale 175 with non-chord tones)**: a long excerpt, and
  non-chord tones are another chapter's topic.
- **Chromatic alterations in figures (16.5)**: only the most common, a ♯
  alone raising the 3rd, is taught (with a check); slashed figures (6 with a
  stroke) and ♭/♮ figures are left out.
- **16.4 says "four situations" but describes three besides the cadential
  one**; the lessons keep that list. Minor-key six-fours (i6/4) are not
  drawn; the lessons keep to major keys.

## Framework gaps hit

None. Stacked labels (`'6\n4'`, three lines for `'7\n5\n3'` and
`'V\n6\n5'`) and `{ vi, en }` labels (`I/3` / `I/3rd`) covered everything.
Labels stack under the note, so a numeral with figures reads top to bottom
(I, 6, 4) rather than with the figures superscript beside the numeral as in
the book.
