What each teaches:

1. **Elements of music**: sections, and contrast by varying the eight elements
   (the book's table); melody (lives, has variety) against ostinato; harmony as
   key and mode (the same tune in {C} major and {C} minor); rhythm as the most
   common note value. Checks: melody or ostinato; {B} in {C} natural minor (pad);
   the most common value on a staff.
2. **More elements**: timbre (tone colors of instruments, and of one
   instrument); texture's vertical plane (number of voices) and horizontal plane
   (rhythmic activity), one voice against four; articulation, staccato against
   legato; dynamics; register (the same tune low and high). Checks: the thicker
   section (ear); staccato (ear); {E4} an octave up.
3. **Rondo**: Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik K. 525, II, a five-part rondo
   (ABACA) with transitions; the book's three tables: A (C major, eighth notes
   after a quarter-note start, top two instruments against a simple bass, legato,
   soft, middle register), B (sixteenth runs, all four voices in one rhythm and
   four staccato notes at each subphrase start), C (C minor, sixteenth-note
   accompaniment, the turn as four thirty-second notes, middle parts paired and
   separated, violin I and bass in imitation). Checks: which section returns; B's
   opening notes; C's key.
4. **Verse and chorus**: replaces 15.3 with a song of our own over I–vi–IV–V: a
   thin verse (melody and bass), a chorus higher and thicker on the same
   progression; voices added and dropped between sections (verse 2 adds bass and
   drums, the pre-chorus lightens); a 3+3+2 post-chorus; the progression changes
   very little, so the other elements mark the form. Checks: what changes from
   verse to chorus; the 3+3+2 grouping; what changes least.
5. **Review**: an ostinato, the third of {C} minor's tonic (pad), the most
   common value (sixteenths), voices in a texture, legato by ear, the rondo's
   form, the A section's dynamics, making a chorus stand out.

## Left out, and why

- **15.3, the song "Rude" by MAGIC!** (copyrighted): not named, quoted or
  analysed. Lesson 4 teaches the same ideas (thin verse, verse 2 adding bass and
  drums, a lighter pre-chorus, a higher chorus, a 3+3+2 post-chorus, a
  progression that hardly changes) with a verse and chorus written for the
  lesson. Its formal diagram is left out.
- **The Mozart score figures** (15.2.1, 15.2.3, 15.2.5, 15.2.7) are images. The
  lessons describe each section from the book's tables and show short examples
  of our own in the same style (block chords, sixteenth runs, a minor-key
  imitation); none is a quotation of the score. The formal diagram (15.2.7)
  becomes the ABACA text and a tiny rondo of our own to hear.
- Timbre and dynamics cannot be heard: lessons play one sampled piano at one
  volume. They are explained in text only, with a tip to listen to real
  recordings.
- Staccato dots cannot be drawn on the staff; staccato is played (short notes
  and rests) and the dot is described in a tip.
- The book's link to "the previous chapter" on texture is kept as a definition
  (vertical and horizontal planes), not a cross-reference.

## Framework gaps hit

- No articulation marks (staccato dots) in the notation.
- No dynamics or instrument choice in play blocks (one piano, one volume).
- A play block is one stream of events, so two voices in different rhythms
  (a melody over moving accompaniment) cannot be played or drawn; examples use
  block chords with the melody on top, and imitation is shown one voice after
  the other.
