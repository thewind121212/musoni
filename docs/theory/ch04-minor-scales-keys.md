# Chapter 4 — Gam thứ và hóa biểu giọng thứ / Minor scales and key signatures

Folder: `web/src/theory/content/ch04-minor-scales-keys/` (chapter id `minor-scales-keys`).
Book: Hutchinson, *Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom*, sections 3.1-3.3,
with the review modelled on the practice exercises (3.4). Assumes app chapters 1-3
(pitch and staff; accidentals and steps; the major scale, major key signatures and
the circle of fifths).

What each teaches:

1. **The natural minor scale**: the three minor scales by name; the natural minor
   as the major scale with degrees 3, 6 and 7 lowered a half step (C major against
   C minor, as the book does); A natural minor on the white keys with its half
   steps at 2-3 and 5-6; writing a natural minor from the major scale on the same
   note (D). Checks: which degrees are lowered; the 6th of C natural minor (pad);
   the 3rd of G natural minor (pad); F major or F natural minor, read and heard.
2. **Harmonic and melodic minor**: harmonic minor raises the natural minor's 7th
   (against major, only 3 and 6 lowered); melodic minor up raises 6 and 7 (only 3
   lowered), down is the natural minor; the three side by side, each playable.
   Checks: the 7th of C harmonic minor (pad); where ascending melodic differs from
   major; which A minor scale (melodic) is on the staff.
3. **Minor key signatures**: the signature agrees with the natural minor (C minor:
   three flats in the order of flats); parallel keys (same tonic; add three flats,
   that is take away three sharps: E major four sharps, E minor one); relative keys
   (same signature; the relative major three half steps above: Eb, D, Db, C); the
   harmonic and melodic minor need accidentals over the minor signature (D minor
   with C#, and B♮ C#). Checks: D minor's signature from D major's; the minor key
   of a two-sharp signature (pad); the accidental G harmonic minor adds (pad).
4. **Scale degree names**: tonic, dominant (a 5th above) and mediant (midway);
   subdominant (a 5th below) and submediant (midway below), the book's reason for
   "sub-"; supertonic and leading tone (a half step below the tonic); the subtonic
   (a whole step below, natural minor). Checks: the dominant of G major, the
   leading tone of F major (pad); what C is in D natural minor.
5. **Review** (after 3.4's exercises 1, 3 and 4): the minor key of a signature
   (two flats; four sharps in bass clef), which A minor scale (harmonic), B minor's
   signature, F major's relative minor, and three "key from a scale degree name"
   questions (Ab the mediant of a minor key, B the dominant of a major key, C the
   submediant of a major key): six answered on the pad, two by choices.

All examples are our own (scales, a key signature alone, two-note steps); no
music or figures from the book.

## Practice links

- 4.3 and the review use Hóa biểu level 4 (major or relative minor from the
  signature), the only drill that asks minor keys.
- 4.4 uses Nghe & Đàn level 3: hearing each degree of a major scale against the
  key is the ear side of naming degrees. Nghe & Đàn is major-only, so it does not
  fit 4.1-4.3.
- 4.1 and 4.2 have none: nothing trains spelling or hearing minor scales yet.

## Left out, and why

- 3.2's three score excerpts (Bach's C minor Invention and C minor fugue from
  WTC I, Mozart's Symphony No. 40): public domain, but too long for a phone step
  and drawn as images in the book; one tip says composers mix the three scales.
- 3.2's uppercase/lowercase shorthand (C for major, c for minor under the staff):
  the app always writes "trưởng" / "thứ" and has no key labels under the staff.
- 3.2's table of all minor signatures and the minor circle of fifths figure: the
  Hóa biểu drill covers all fifteen; one tip ties relative minors to the circle.
- 3.4 exercise 2 and 3 ("write the signature and scale"): writing on the staff is
  not something a check can take; the review asks the same facts as choices.
- 3.4 exercise 4's keys with a Cb or E# answer (Bb is the leading tone, E# the
  supertonic): Cb and E# are not on the answer pad.

## Source notes

The pages were fetched and read as text (3.1 `MinorScales.html`, 3.2
`MinorKeySignatures.html`, 3.3 `ScaleDegreeNames.html`, 3.4
`MinorKeysPracticeExercises.html`), including the figures' alt text, which carries
most of 3.1 and 3.3 (C major against the three minor scales; the degree names; the
"5th above / 5th below" picture). The lessons follow the book's order and its
framing of minor against the major scale on the same note.

- Added for a phone, not on those pages: A natural minor on the white keys with its
  half steps, and building a natural minor from the major scale on a second note (D).
- The book gives the relative major as three half steps above the minor; the
  lessons count down from the major (Eb, D, Db, C), as its figure does.
