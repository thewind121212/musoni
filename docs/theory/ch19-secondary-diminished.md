No lesson has a practice link: Hợp âm (`chords`) asks diatonic triads only, and
nothing trains seventh chords or secondary chords. Every end screen leads with
"Bài tiếp".

The chapter title is "Hợp âm giảm phụ", not "Hợp âm bảy giảm phụ": the book's
secondary diminished chords include the triad vii°/x, not only the sevenths,
and the name pairs with "hợp âm át phụ" (secondary dominant, chapter 18).

What each teaches:

1. **What a secondary diminished chord is**: vii° has dominant function like V,
   so, like a secondary dominant, it can tonicize a chord other than I; the
   three kinds vii°/x, viiø7/x, vii°7/x, the numeral after the slash being the
   chord tonicized; the root is a half step below the target's root; the
   chromatic note is a chord member ("essential" chromaticism), shown in our own
   ascending chromatic bass I, vii°7/ii, ii, vii°7/iii, iii in C major (in
   place of the book's sequence figure 18.1.1). Checks: how far F♯ is from G;
   F♯ A C E in C major (viiø7/V); where vii°7/ii resolves.
2. **In major**: the chords of ii, iii, IV, V and vi in C major (vii° is not
   tonicized, being diminished); minor triads take vii°7; major triads
   traditionally viiø7, also vii°7 (viiø7/V vs vii°7/V differ only in the
   seventh). Checks: root of vii°7/vi in C (pad); which seventh tonicizes ii;
   root of viiø7/V in G (pad).
3. **In minor**: the chords of iv, V, VI, VII in A minor; no vii°/III (it is
   ii°); viiø7/III is spelled like iiø7 but goes to III, while iiø7 is
   pre-dominant and goes to V. Checks: root of vii°7/iv in A minor (pad);
   B D F A resolving to C E G; root of vii°7/V in D minor (pad).
4. **Analyzing**: the book's two steps: stack in thirds for root and quality
   (only °, ø7, °7 go on), then the note a minor 2nd above the root names the
   target; inversions take figures (viiø65/V, our C♯ø7/E in G major in place
   of the book's G♯ø7/B in D major). Checks: root of E G B C♯; D♯ F♯ A C in C
   (vii°7/iii); B♮ D F A♭ in F (vii°7/V); D F♯ A C in C is V7/V, not a
   secondary diminished chord.
5. **Writing**: the book's steps: the root of the chord after the slash, a
   minor 2nd below it, build the chord with the accidentals the key signature
   lacks, invert by the figures (vii°42: the seventh in the bass); our vii°7/ii
   and vii°42/ii in F major in place of the book's vii°42/iv in G minor. Checks: root of vii°7/vi in F (pad); the
   seventh of vii°7/ii in F (pad); bass of viiø43/V in G (pad).
6. **Review**: analyze G♯ B D F in D major, E♮ G B♭ D in B♭ major and
   B♭ C♯ E G in C major (vii°42/ii); write the root of vii°7/iv in D minor, the
   seventh of vii°7/V in A minor, the bass of viiø65/V in D major; viiø7/III
   equals iiø7 in E minor. Modelled on 18.5's exercises 1 (analyze) and 2
   (write).

Roman numerals are plain text; figures are written after the numeral in text
(viiø65/V, vii°42/ii) and stacked under the note on the staff (`'viiø65\n/V'`),
since a one-line label is at most 6 characters. All examples are our own; no
music or figures from the book.

## Terms not in the glossary

Chosen for this chapter, used consistently (the glossary rows are for the main
agent to add; the first two follow chapter 18):

| Tiếng Việt | English |
|---|---|
| hợp âm át phụ | secondary dominant |
| chủ âm hóa | tonicization (tonicize) |
| hợp âm giảm phụ | secondary diminished chord |

"Hợp âm được chủ âm hóa" / "the chord being tonicized" describes the target;
"nốt ngoài giọng" / "a note from outside the key" stands in for the book's
"chromaticism" without a new term. Inversion figures (65, 43, 42) and "thế đảo
1" assume chapter 17 (figured bass).

## Left out, and why

- 18.1's four excerpts: Bach's BWV 147 chorale and Tchaikovsky's Trepak are
  public domain but need score excerpts longer than a phone step; the two pop
  songs are copyrighted. Their point (real secondary diminished chords, the
  chromatic note as a chord member) is carried by our own chromatic-bass
  progression in lesson 1.
- 18.5 exercise 3 (a 1977 rock song): copyrighted; not used, and its answer
  key was not copied. Exercises 1 and 2 shape the review.
- Lead-sheet symbols for every chord (the exercises ask for them above the
  staff): lessons name a few (C♯ø7) but checks ask Roman numerals and notes,
  the chapter's subject.
- viiø7/V in minor (D♯ F♯ A C♯ in A minor): the lessons show vii°7/V, the
  usual choice; the general rule (major triads take viiø7 or vii°7) covers it.
- Voice leading of the resolutions (the seventh falls, the root rises) beyond
  what the staves show: the book's chapter does not teach it.
- Ear checks: no check type asks a reader to tell the chords apart by ear;
  every step plays its chords instead.

## Source notes

The five pages were fetched and their text read in full (18.1
`SecondaryDiminishedChordSection.html`, 18.2

`AnalyzingSecondaryDiminishedChords.html`, 18.4

`SecondaryDiminishedPracticeExercises.html`). Every figure is an image with
empty alt text (the sequence in C major, the tables of secondary diminished
chords in major and minor, the analysis and writing steps, the exercises), so
their contents were rebuilt from the prose and standard theory: in major,
vii°7/ii, vii°7/iii, viiø7 and vii°7 of IV and V, vii°7/vi; in minor, vii°7/iv,
vii°7/V, viiø7 (or vii°7) of VI and VII, viiø7/III; the root of each is a
minor 2nd below the target's root.
