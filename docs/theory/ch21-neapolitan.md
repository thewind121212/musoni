No lesson has a practice link: the Hợp âm drill (`chords`) asks diatonic
triads only, so nothing trains ♭II. Every end screen leads with "Bài tiếp".

What each teaches:

1. **What the Neapolitan is**: ♭II, a major triad on the lowered second degree
   of a minor key (D♭ F A♭ beside ii° D F A♭ in C minor); labelled N; most
   common in first inversion, N6, whose bass is scale degree 4; its lead-sheet
   symbol (D♭/F); a chromatically altered ii, so pre-dominant function, going
   to V (i–N6–V–i in C minor). Checks: the root of N in A minor (B♭, pad); the
   bass of N6 in E minor (A, pad); its function.
2. **From the Neapolitan to V**: the lowered second falls a diminished third
   to the leading tone (D♭–B–C); N6 often passes through i6/4 to V7; the
   book's point that composers change texture, register or dynamics at the
   Neapolitan (it cites Beethoven's Seventh Symphony), shown with our own
   D minor phrase where N6 leaps into a higher register. Checks: where the
   lowered second falls in A minor (G♯, pad); what composers change; an
   ear-only i–N6–V–i in D minor (iv or N6).
3. **The Neapolitan in music**: the opening of Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata
   (op. 27 no. 2, I) as a simplified chord plan, i–VI–N6–V7–i in C♯ minor;
   the book's suggestion to swap N6 for iv (F♯m) or ii°6 (D♯°/F♯), with three
   play buttons; N in a major key, which also needs the lowered sixth
   borrowed from the parallel minor (I–N6–V–I in C major). Checks: the root
   of N in C♯ minor (D, pad); the second chord of a read-and-heard A minor
   progression (N6 among iv and ii°6); the notes of N6 in G major.
4. **Review**, after 20.3's two exercises (analyze chords with symbols and
   numerals; spell a chord from its numeral): E♭ G B♭ over G in D minor (N6),
   the root of N in G minor (A♭, pad), the symbol for N6 in E minor (F/A), the
   bass of N6 in B minor (E, pad), why N is a pre-dominant, an F minor
   i–N6–V–i (read and heard), and what follows N6.

Progressions are four parts on a grand staff (bass below, three voices
above), with the key signature drawn and a "Nghe" button. Inversion figures
are written inline (N6, V7, ii°6) except i6/4, stacked as `i\n6\n4`.

## Terms not in the glossary

Chosen for this chapter, used consistently (rows for the main agent to add):

| Tiếng Việt | English |
|---|---|
| hợp âm Napoli (N, N6) | Neapolitan chord |
| bậc 2 hạ (♭2) | lowered second scale degree (♭2̂) |
| biến âm (hợp âm ii đã biến âm) | chromatically altered (chord) |

Also used from chapters being written alongside: hợp âm mượn (borrowed
chord). The title "Hợp âm Napoli" is kept; Vietnamese texts also say "hợp âm
sáu Napoli" for N6, but the plain name matches the book's heading.

## Left out, and why

- **Every copyrighted example in 20.2**: the Raiders March, the Star Trek end
  credits and the Batman v Superman cue (film music, all under copyright). The
  book's point they carry (try other pre-dominants in place of N, notice the
  effect) is kept with our own C♯ minor and A minor progressions.
- **The Beethoven Seventh Symphony figure (20.1)**: public domain, but an
  orchestral score excerpt too long for a phone. The lesson keeps the book's
  sentence about texture, register and dynamics and shows it with our own
  D minor phrase.
- **The Moonlight Sonata figure** is replaced by a simplified chord plan
  (measures 1, 3-5, block chords instead of the triplet arpeggios; measure 2's
  i over B is left out). Beethoven (died 1827), published 1802: public domain.
- **Exercise images in 20.3** are not reproduced; the review asks the same
  two tasks (name the chord with numeral and symbol; spell a chord from N6)
  in keys of our choice.

Added from standard theory, not in the book's short text: the diminished-third
fall of ♭2 to the leading tone, the N6–i6/4–V path, and N in major keys with
the borrowed lowered sixth. Each is one step; they explain the progressions
the book's figures show.
