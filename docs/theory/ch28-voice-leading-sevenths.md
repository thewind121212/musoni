No lesson has a practice link: no drill trains seventh chords or voice
leading (the Hợp âm drill asks triads only). Every end screen leads with "Bài
tiếp".

What each teaches:

1. **The seventh resolves down**: the one general rule (resolve the seventh
   down by step; the other voices move smoothly to the nearest chord tone with
   the right doubling), shown on V7–I in C major; the same rule for every
   seventh chord (ii7–V7–I, both sevenths falling); the one exception,
   I–V4/3–I6 with the bass rising C–D–E, where the seventh rises and the
   unequal fifths B–F to C–G are accepted (the book cites the close of the
   theme of Mozart's K.331, first movement; named in text only). Checks: where
   the seventh of D7 goes in G major (B, pad); spot the error in a V7–I whose
   seventh rises; how the seventh moves in I–V4/3–I6.
2. **Successive seventh chords**: in a circle of fifths of root-position
   sevenths in four parts, alternate complete and incomplete (no fifth) chords
   (vi7–ii7–V7–I in C); with inverted sevenths, each seventh falls and two
   voices hold common tones while two step down (vi4/2–ii6/5–V4/2–I6); a tip
   for the 7 / 4/3 alternation. Checks: which note ii7 leaves out; what the
   voices do from ii6/5 to V4/2; where the seventh of ii6/5 goes in G major
   (F♯, pad).
3. **From V7 to I**: strict resolution, complete V7 to incomplete I (tripled
   root, one third) and incomplete V7 to complete I; free resolution, the
   leading tone not in the soprano dropping a third to 5; the piano exception
   (a tip; the book cites Giordani's *Caro mio ben*, named in text only); 27.3.1,
   I6/4 to V7, moving the upper voices up when the fifth is above the root.
   Checks: the shape of I after a complete V7; when the leading tone may drop;
   which pair of voices makes fifths in a row when I6/4's upper voices all fall
   to V7 (tenor and soprano, C–G to B–F).
4. **Resolving vii°7 and viiø7**: they are all tendency tones (7, 2, 4, 6) and
   each takes its path; with the seventh below the third, every tone resolves
   normally (viiø7–I in C); with the seventh a fifth above the third, the third
   (2) rises to 3 to avoid parallel fifths, giving I a doubled third; the
   book's revoicing alternative (parallel fourths are fine) as a tip. Then
   27.5: sevenths add tension approaching the tonic, so they go mostly on
   dominant (V, vii°) and pre-dominant (ii, less IV) chords; iii7, vi7, IVM7,
   IM7 are less common (I–ii6/5–V7–I). Checks: where 4 goes (E, pad); spot the
   parallel fifths in a viiø7–I; which seventh chord is least common.
5. **Review**, after 27.6's three exercises (voice-lead V7–I strict or free;
   a circle of fifths in root position; one with inverted sevenths): the
   seventh of A7 in D major (F♯, pad); strict or free in an F major V7–I (read
   and heard); the seventh of ii7 in B♭ major (A, pad); where 2 goes in a
   G major viiø7 with the seventh above the third (B, pad); how to voice a
   root-position circle of fifths; the I–V4/3–I6 exception; which functions
   take sevenths.

All examples are SATB on a grand staff, soprano and alto on the treble, tenor
and bass on the bass (`@b` on a tenor at or above middle C, `@t` on an alto
below it), each with a "Nghe" button. Inversion figures are stacked in staff
labels (`V\n4\n3`) and inline in text (V4/3). Every example was checked by
hand for parallel fifths and octaves, spacing (an octave or less between
upper voices) and doubling, except where a check shows an error on purpose.

## Left out, and why

- **The book's figures** (Beethoven's *Pathétique* II, Schubert's
  *Frühlingstraum*, Mozart K.331 I, Vivaldi RV 565, Giordani's *Caro mio ben*,
  Bach's chorale BWV 376, and the exercise images): all public domain, but
  they are images with empty alt text and score excerpts too long for a
  phone. Every idea they show is drawn with our own four-part examples in C,
  G, F, D and B♭ major; Mozart and Giordani are named in text where the book
  makes a point with them.
- **27.2's second figure** (alternating 7 and 4/3 chords) is a tip, not a
  staff: it shows the same pattern as the 6/5 and 4/2 chain.
- **27.4's minor-key vii°7**: the lessons show viiø7 in C major; the rule is
  the same and the text names both chords.
- **27.6's worksheet tasks** (realize a figured bass in four parts) cannot be
  answered on the pad or with choices; the review asks the decisions those
  exercises need (where a seventh goes, strict or free, complete or
  incomplete).
- No copyrighted music: none is quoted in this chapter of the book.

## Framework gaps hit

- None blocking. A check cannot ask the reader to write a voice on the staff,
  so "spot the error" and "which resolution" are choice checks.
