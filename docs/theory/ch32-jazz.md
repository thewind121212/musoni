No drill trains seventh or extended chords, progressions or scales over
chords (port guide §4: the Hợp âm drill asks triads only), so no lesson has a
practice link; each end screen leads with "Bài tiếp".

What each teaches:

1. **Ninth chords**: the 9th is degree 2 an octave up, from the major scale;
   a 9 in a symbol implies the 7th (C7 → C9); maj/Δ is about the 7th, m/– about
   the 3rd (Cmaj9, C9, Cm9); four of the five seventh chord types take a 9th,
   not the fully diminished; Cø7 = Cm7(♭5); the minor-major seventh CmΔ7;
   add 9 when there is no 7th; altered numbers in parentheses, C7(♭9). Checks:
   the 9th of G9 (pad); which seventh never gets a 9th; the notes of Cmaj9.
2. **Elevenths, thirteenths and altered dominants**: 11 = 4 and 13 = 6 an
   octave up; every number below the highest is assumed (C13), and the book's
   own C9(13) for leaving out the 11th; "6" only without a 7th, else "13";
   "sus" only without a 3rd, else "11" (C6, C7sus); ♯11 not ♭5 when a normal
   5th is present; 6/9 and m6/9 chords (not slash chords); altered dominants
   (♯5 respelled ♭ to agree with the 7th, ♯9 written as ♭10, altered when the
   root is about to fall a fifth). Checks: C E G B♭ A is C7(13); the ♭9 of
   G7(♭9) (pad); F♯ in C E G B♭ F♯ is ♯11.
3. **Writing and analyzing**: start from every major/perfect note up to the
   13th; writing GmΔ11 against G11; respelling A7(♯5♯9) to A C♯ F G C; the
   four analysis steps, worked on G B D F B♭ = G7(♯9). Checks: the 7th of
   AmΔ9 (pad); G in E G♯ B D G is ♯9 (not a minor 3rd, not ♭11); F B♭ E♭ G
   is F9sus.
4. **Guide tones, voicings, II–V–I**: guide tones (3rd and 7th) move by step
   in Dm7–G7–Cmaj7; spread and close five-note voicings of C9(13) on the
   keyboard, with the sus/6 and drop-the-5th rules; II–V–I in major
   (Dm9–G9(13)–Cmaj9) and minor (Dø7–G7alt–Cm6/9), the book's chords, in our
   own close voicings; the III–VI–II–V turnaround, all-dominant E7–A7–D7–G7
   and its tritone substitution E7–E♭7–D7–D♭7, G7 and D♭7 sharing guide
   tones. Checks: guide tones of A7; II–V–I in F; tritone substitute of D7
   (pad).
5. **Blues and jazz scales**: a 12-measure jazz blues in F of our own, played
   as roots and guide tones; the blues scale 1–♭3–4–♭5–5–♭7 and blue notes; the
   bebop dominant scale (Mixolydian plus a passing major 7th) in descending
   eighths; finding a chord's scale by filling gaps without augmented 2nds or
   consecutive half steps (Gmaj7(♯11) → G Lydian) and the common chord-scale
   pairs; the bebop major scale harmonized with C6 and vii°7 chords. Checks:
   the ♭3 of the A blues scale (pad); the scale for C7.
6. **Review** (after the book's five "Day" exercise groups): the 13th of F13
   and the ♭9 of D7(♭9) (writing chords); naming Am9 and C9sus (labeling
   chords); guide tones of B♭7 and the tritone substitute of C7 (voice
   leading); naming a G blues scale; the scale for Fmaj7(♯11).

## Left out, and why

- **Every song the book names or quotes**: the standards listed under II–V–I
  (31.8.1), the rock riff for the blues scale (31.9.1), the standard used for
  the vi7 chord-scale example (31.10), and the big-band recording in 31.11.
  All are copyrighted; the lessons use our own progressions, voicings and
  scales, and the 31.10 point (the key of the tune can change the scale) is
  left out with its example.
- **31.4 Chord labels**: the book's table is condensed from a published fake
  book, and is a picture list of symbol spellings. The lessons teach the
  spelling rules instead and use our own symbols throughout.
- **The full table of scales (31.9.3) and most of the chord-scale list
  (31.10.1)**: about twenty modes and synthetic scales do not fit a phone
  lesson. Lesson 5 keeps the method and five common pairs (major/Lydian,
  Dorian/natural minor, Mixolydian, half-whole octatonic, altered); the
  melodic-minor modes, whole-tone pairings and the remaining rows are out.
- **The C7(13)–F7(♯9) chromatic guide-tone exercise and the tritone
  guide-tone cycle (31.7.1)**: keyboard practice, not a concept; the shared
  guide tones of a tritone pair are taught in lesson 4.
- **♭II7 for V7 in a full progression and the D♭9(13) for G7(♯9♯5) example
  (31.8.2)**, the bebop major scale **in minor** (31.11 figure 2), and the
  book's two blues choruses with written spread and close voicings (31.8.3):
  kept to one sentence or one example each to stay within lesson length.
- **G7alt as vague, ♭13 vs ♯5 (31.3)**, **C+7 vs C♭9 vs C(♭9) (31.1)**, and
  add –6 / C♯–6: fine print left out.
- **Writing chords, voice-leading and writing scales (exercises 1, 3, 5)**:
  the app cannot write notes on a staff; the review asks single notes and
  names instead.

## Framework notes

- **No simultaneous notes across a grand staff.** A chord event sits on one
  staff, so a bass note with a right-hand chord cannot be drawn together.
  Voicings are shown on the keyboard (`keys`) and as right-hand chords on a
  treble staff, with the root added in the `play` block.
- **No stacked alterations or superscripts in labels.** Chord symbols are
  written on one line, `A7(♯5♯9)`, `C6/9`; labels over 6 characters
  (`F13sus(♭9)`, `Dm7(♭5)`) are avoided (`Dø7` is used, as the book allows).
- A section must look like `31.8`, so lesson 4 and lesson 5 both cite 31.8
  (its subsections 31.8.1–2 and 31.8.3).

## Source notes

Read from the book pages themselves (31.1 `JazzChordBasics.html` through
31.11 `harmonizing-bebop-scale.html`, and 31.12 `JazzExercises.html`) and
compared lesson by lesson: concepts, order and the book's rules follow the
text. The figures are images without useful alt text, so the chords on the
staff are written from the rules in the text and standard jazz theory: the
II–V–I chords are the book's symbols in our own voicings; the bebop
harmonization is the standard C6 / vii°7 method the text describes; the blues
is a common jazz blues form with our own guide-tone voicing. Worked examples
were re-rooted (the book's DmΔ11 → GmΔ11, E7(♯5♯9♯11) → A7(♯5♯9),
E♭7(♯11♯9) and F13sus(♭9) → G7(♯9) and F9sus) so the lessons adapt rather
than copy.
