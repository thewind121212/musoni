Lessons 1 and 2 link to Quãng level 1 (interval size): labelling every
interval between the voices is the core skill of species counterpoint. No
drill trains suspensions, inventions or fugues, so lessons 3-5 and the review
have no practice link.

What each teaches:

1. **First species**: counterpoint as lines sounding together; contrapuntal
   against homophonic texture; Fux's Gradus ad Parnassum (1725) and the
   species; the cantus firmus; note against note; interval numbers under the
   notes (compounds reduced); allowed leaps, no repeated notes; consonance
   and dissonance, perfect and imperfect, at most three 3rds or 6ths in a row;
   begin and end on an octave, 7–8 against 2–1 in contrary motion; perfect
   consonances only by contrary motion (parallel and direct fifths/octaves);
   tip: the cantus firmus on top, the raised leading tone in minor. Checks: a
   6th between the voices; the next-to-last note (B, pad); a direct fifth.
2. **Second and third species**: two half notes, the first consonant, the
   second consonant or a passing tone; starting on beat 3 after a half rest;
   rules across the bar line (no repeated notes, no octaves/5ths on
   consecutive downbeats); the 5–6 ending (and 5–3 with the cantus firmus on
   top, in a tip); four quarters, beat 1 consonant, passing tones on beats
   2-4; the 4–5–6–7 and cambiata (2–1–6–7) endings; direct fifths/octaves.
   Checks: a passing tone; degree 4 starting the ending (F, pad); a
   dissonance on beat 1.
3. **Fourth and fifth species**: half rest, then ties across the bar line;
   the dissonant tied note is a suspension resolving down by step, 4–3 and
   7–6 only (no 9–8, 2–1); the consonant one a syncopation; beat 3 always
   consonant; the 7–6 ending; fifth (florid) species as a free mix, naming
   Lasso's Expectatio Justorum as the book does. Checks: a 7–6 suspension;
   resolving E over F (D, pad); which species a measure of quarters borrows.
4. **Invention expositions**: Bach's two-part inventions as models; I–I–V–V–I;
   the theme in measure 1, an octave lower in the bass in measure 2, a perfect
   5th higher in measure 3, measure 3 an octave lower in measure 4, the final
   10th; counterpoint that brings out the harmony, microharmonies (tip);
   altering the theme at measures 4-5; in minor, 2 and ♭3 up a 5th become
   raised 6 and 7. Checks: the measure-3 start (G, pad); the final 10th;
   ♭3 of E minor up a 5th (D♯, pad).
5. **Fugue analysis**: fugue, exposition, subject; the answer in the dominant,
   real and tonal (our subject leaps a 4th, the tonal answer a 5th, as the
   book's Bach example does); countersubject and CTRPT; episodes,
   fragmentation, sequence, head and tail motives; the bridge. Checks: real
   or tonal answer (two staves); what a countersubject is; an episode.
6. **Review**: the book's exercises are writing and analysis tasks, turned
   into checks: find the dissonant measure in first species, parallel
   fifths, where second species allows a dissonance, the consonant beat in
   third species, resolving a 7–6 suspension (E, pad), allowed suspensions,
   an invention theme in F up a 5th (C, pad), the key of the answer.

All examples are our own, in C major (A and E minor for the transposition
rule): cantus firmi, counterpoints, the invention theme and the fugue subject.

## Notation

First species and the invention/fugue examples are drawn on a grand staff,
note against note. Species 2-5 have a different rhythm in each voice, which
one staff event cannot hold, so the counterpoint (treble, interval numbers
under it) and the cantus firmus (bass) are two staves one above the other;
their measures line up closely but not exactly at phone width. Suspensions
and syncopations carry stacked labels (`7\nsus`, `5\nsync`, the book's
abbreviations, the same in both languages).

  writing exercises (1-5) become checks on the same rules.

## Framework gaps hit

- **No independent voices.** A staff event has one duration across the grand
  staff, so a whole-note cantus firmus cannot sound under two or four notes.
  Species 2-5 use two staves (see Notation); invention and fugue
  counterpoint moves note against note with the theme.
- **Playback strikes each pair together**, so the cantus firmus is struck
  again under every counterpoint note, and a tied suspension is struck
  again (a tie on a chord token ties the whole chord and silences the next
  event, as chapter 11 found).
- No drill trains species rules, suspensions or fugue terms.
