The Roman numeral levels of Hợp âm train the skill every lesson leans on (naming
a diatonic triad's numeral in a key); they do not train progressions themselves.

What each teaches:

1. **Circle of fifths**: harmonic progression; I–IV–vii°–iii–vi–ii–V–I with roots
   falling by fifths (or rising by fourths) on diatonic notes only, so F–B is a
   diminished fifth; the minor form i–iv–VII–III–VI–ii°–V–i with the subtonic VII
   and the harmonic-minor V; a sequence, and the circle as a harmonic sequence.
   Checks: the chord after vi; the root after VII in A minor (pad); what a sequence is.
2. **Shorter progressions**: ii–V–I; vi–ii–V–I and its rotations I–vi–ii–V and
   ii–V–I–vi (vi as a link to the next phrase); iii–vi–ii–V as a loop, rotated to
   ii–V–iii–vi; harmonic rhythm (four beats vs two beats a chord). Checks: ii in G
   (pad); a rotation; the harmonic rhythm of a staff.
3. **Harmonic function**: the book's four functions: tonic (I), dominant (V,
   vii°: leading tone and supertonic), pre-dominant (IV, ii: degrees 4 and 6),
   tonic prolongation (vi, iii: two common tones with I); the flowchart in major
   (I → iii → vi → IV/ii → V/vii° → I, I to anything) and in minor (VII only to
   III). Checks: vii°'s function; vi's function; what follows ii.
4. **Progressions by function**: T–D–T (I–V–I, I–vii°–I); T–PD–D–T in its four
   forms (the vii° ones rarer); T–TP–PD–D as I–vi–IV–V (the '50s progression)
   and I–vi–ii/3rd–V on the same bass; the exceptions (IV–I as tonic
   prolongation, V–vi, I/5th–V as dominant function); exceptions made by
   sequences. Checks: which is T–PD–D–T; the last bass note of I–vi–IV–V (pad);
   which pair repeats I–V's pattern.
5. **Popular music**: ♭VII in major (before any function but tonic; ♭VII–IV–I);
   the best-seller I–V–vi–IV and its rotations; vi–IV–I–V heard as
   i–VI–III–VII in the relative minor; i–VII–VI–VII against the Andalusian
   progression (ends on V), its rotation VI–VII–i–VII and major reading
   vi–V–IV–V. Checks: ♭VII in G (pad); C major's numeral heard in A minor; the
   Andalusian's last root (pad).
6. **Review**: after 9.9's exercises: the circle in F major and B minor (pad,
   from exercise 1), iii's and a pre-dominant's function (exercise 7), the half
   cadence's chord (exercise 5), harmonic rhythm, a progression in E major to
   analyse (exercise 6), the best-seller.

All examples are our own: block chords in C major, A minor and E major, and a
three-note melodic sequence written for the lesson.

## Left out, and why

- **Every song and score from the book.** 9.1-9.8 illustrate with pop songs,
  jazz standards, film and TV music and score excerpts (Bach, Handel, Mozart,
  Beethoven and others). The pop and jazz ones are copyrighted, so none is named,
  quoted or linked; the classical ones need long score excerpts. The progressions
  themselves are generic and are shown in our own voicings.
- The book's figures (the circle and flowchart diagrams): no images. The
  flowchart is written as a line of numerals with arrows; a drawn diagram would
  need a block type the framework does not have.
- 9.6's open questions (does IV have dominant function when it goes to I in pop
  music? is ♭VII then "pre pre-dominant"?): discussion, not facts to check.
- 9.8's VI–VII–i–i variant (fourth chord dropped): one rotation is enough on a
  phone.
- The jazz "turnaround" (9.3) beyond one tip: the book defers it to a later
  chapter.
- 9.9's review exercises on intervals, rhythm notation and seventh chords:
  earlier chapters' material.
- Book order: 9.2 (harmonic rhythm) is taught at the end of lesson 2, after the
  circle segments it describes, so its examples can be those progressions.

## Framework gaps hit

- A chord cannot be split across a grand staff (bass note below, triad above):
  `NoteStaff` puts a whole chord event on one staff. Progressions are drawn as
  treble triads; the bass is heard in the play blocks (root doubled an octave
  or two below) and drawn alone on a bass staff where it matters (lesson 1).
- No drill trains progressions or harmonic function, so three lessons have no
  practice link.
