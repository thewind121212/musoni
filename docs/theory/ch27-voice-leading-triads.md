No drill trains voice leading. Only 27.4 links to practice: its doubling
rules depend on spotting the inversion first, which Hợp âm level 4 trains
(as in 7.3 and 17.1). The other end screens lead with "Bài tiếp".

What each teaches:

1. **Four voices and types of motion**: voice leading (part writing) and its
   aims (independent parts, good voicing, singable lines); SATB; chorale style
   (S+A on the treble, T+B on the bass) against piano style (three voices on
   the treble); contrary, oblique, similar and parallel motion, parallel as a
   kind of similar. Checks: the alto of a chorale-style chord (F, pad); a
   similar-motion pair (5th to 4th, so not parallel); an oblique pair.
2. **Parallels, ranges and spacing**: avoid parallel fifths and octaves (and
   twelfths, unisons) between any two voices; parallel 3rds, 4ths, 6ths fine,
   2nds and 7ths not used; voice ranges S C4-G5, A G3-D5, T C3-G4, B E2-C4;
   rules of melody (tendency tones resolve, no augmented intervals, change
   direction after a leap larger than a 4th, consecutive leaps outline a
   triad); spacing (S-A and A-T within an octave, T-B may be wider; wide low,
   close high, as in the overtone series). Checks: I-ii with parallel fifths
   and octaves (parallel sixths as the trap); where the leading tone goes in
   G major (G, pad); which of three chords has a spacing error.
3. **Root-position triads**: always double the root; voice-lead by the bass
   interval: a 3rd or 6th (keep two common tones, I-vi), a 4th or 5th (keep
   the common tone, I-IV; or all upper voices the same way, V-I), a 2nd
   (contrary to the bass, IV-V), the deceptive progression (3rd of V up, vi
   doubles its 3rd; always in minor, in major when 7 is in the soprano); a
   repeated bass (tip). Checks: the doubled note of IV in C (F, pad); which
   I-IV in G is right; which IV-V in D is right (the wrong ones move in
   parallel fifths and octaves).
4. **Doubling in inversions**: first inversion, do not double the bass;
   double the root or fifth (I6 both ways); diminished triads in first
   inversion (vii°6, ii°6) double the 3rd; consecutive first-inversion chords
   alternate, and a 3rd approached from opposite directions may be doubled
   (tip); never double the 3rd of V; second inversion doubles the bass; the
   summary of 26.12. Checks: the note IV6 should not double in C (A, pad); the
   note V6 must never double in G (F♯, pad); the doubled note of IV6/4 in F
   (F, pad); which chord doubles its bass (ii°6).
5. **Special situations and six-fours**: roots a 2nd apart (ii6-I6/4,
   I-vii°6) invite parallel fifths; the fix, the fifth below the root in the
   upper voices, which turns them into parallel fourths; the three places to
   write a six-four: pedal (I-IV6/4-I, V-I6/4-V), passing (I-V6/4-I6,
   IV-I6/4-IV6, or reversed), cadential. Checks: which I-vii°6 avoids
   parallel fifths; a passing V6/4 in G; which pattern is a pedal six-four.
6. **Review**, after 26.13's exercises (voice-lead by bass interval, track
   doubling, six-four types): spot parallel fifths and octaves in V-vi; what
   the upper voices do when the bass moves by a 3rd; the doubled note of VI in
   A minor's V-VI (A, pad); a spacing error; a note outside the soprano range;
   the doubled note of vii°6 in D (E, pad); of I6/4 in B♭ (F, pad); parallel
   motion.

Every SATB example is our own, written in chorale style on a grand staff:
soprano and alto on the treble, tenor and bass on the bass, with `@b` on a
tenor at C4 or above and `@t` on an alto below C4. Roman numerals with
figures are stacked labels (`'vii°\n6'`, `'I\n6\n4'`); "choose the right
voicing" checks show two pairs labelled 1 and 2. Every voicing was checked by
hand for doubling, ranges, spacing and parallels (except where a check's
wrong answer has them on purpose).

The title "Dẫn bè hợp âm ba" is kept; "hòa âm bốn bè" is also heard in
Vietnamese classes, but it names four-part harmony in general, not the
book's topic.

## Left out, and why

- **The Bach chorale figures in 26.8 and 26.9** (Chorales 237, 153, 103, 106,
  204) and **the Palestrina Sanctus** (26.8): public domain, but score
  excerpts too long for a phone; each rule is shown with our own short
  progression in C major.
- **The overtone series figure (26.6)** is kept as one sentence; no harmonic
  series is drawn.
- **The two deceptive-progression figures in major (26.7.6-7)**: one example
  (7 in the soprano, vi doubling its 3rd) carries both the minor rule and the
  major case; the other major solution (7 in an inner voice, normal doubling)
  is left out.
- **The 26.5 "hard to sing" melody** is left out; the rules are shown on one
  short line of our own.
- **26.13's writing exercises** (add S, A, T above a bass; harmonize a
  melody; analyze Bach's Chorale 367) become spot-the-error and
  pick-the-voicing checks; the app has no writing check.
- **Six-four types**: 26.11 names three (pedal, passing, cadential);
  chapter 17 also taught the melodic-bass six-four. This chapter follows 26.11
  and does not repeat the cadential six-four beyond one sentence.

The book chapter quotes no copyrighted music (only Bach and Palestrina);
even so, every example here is our own.

## Framework gaps hit

None blocking. A staff cannot highlight one chord member, so a voice in a
chord is named in the text instead (lesson 27.1, check 1 asks for the
alto on the pad). Staff labels are per chord, so the SATB letters are shown
on the four voices written one by one before the full chord. In a "which
voicing" check the two pairs are numbered with labels; there is no way to
mark the parallel voices with lines, as the book's figures do.
