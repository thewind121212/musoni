What each teaches:

1. **Sonata form**: the three sections (exposition, development,
   recapitulation); the exposition's primary theme in tonic, a modulating
   transition, the secondary theme in V (major) or III (minor), an optional
   closing theme; the development's fragmentation, sequences and modulations,
   ending in a retransition over a V pedal; the recapitulation with every theme
   in tonic and only tonicizations in the transition; the Classical repeats
   (exposition, then development and recapitulation together) and the sonata
   as rounded plus balanced binary. Checks: the secondary theme's key in
   {F} major (pad, {C}); which section modulates most; the secondary theme's key
   in the recapitulation.
2. **Sonatina, sonata principle, monothematic sonata**: sonatina form as sonata
   form without a development (slow movements and overtures; Tchaikovsky's
   Miniature Overture named, not quoted); Rosen's sonata principle (material
   outside the tonic as a large-scale dissonance that must be resolved, so the
   secondary theme returns in tonic); Haydn's monothematic sonata (the primary
   theme restated in the dominant). Checks: what sonatina leaves out; how the
   secondary theme is resolved; what Haydn puts in the secondary theme's place.
3. **The four structural functions** (Temko and Spencer): expository (stable
   key, clear melody, defined phrases, cadences mark them), transitional (moves
   key, active accompaniment, tonicizations, often a half cadence and a rest),
   developmental (sequence, fragmentation, many keys, irregular phrases,
   elisions; longer than a transition), terminative (emphatic I-V alternation;
   the closing theme). Checks: name the function of an expository, a
   transitional (ear) and a developmental (ear) passage.
4. **Rondo form**: refrain and episodes; five-part ABACA / ABABA and seven-part
   ABACABA; the key plan (refrain in tonic; B in V or III; C in tonic minor,
   vi or IV, in minor I, VI or iv; retransitions), drawn as a staff of key
   chords with stacked labels (section over Roman numeral); Beethoven's
   Pathétique finale as a key plan only ({C} minor, B in {Eb} major, C in
   {Ab} major, B again in {C} major: the sonata principle); sonata rondo
   (A-B-A-Dev.-A-B-A). Checks: B's key in a {C} minor rondo (pad, {Eb}); {Ab} as
   VI of {C} minor; what sonata rondo replaces.
5. **Rondo character and multimovement works**: quick, duple (2/4 or 6/8),
   light, staccato; common in Classical final movements; form is not
   character. The standard four-movement plan (sonata form; ternary, sonatina or
   five-part rondo; minuet and trio as compound ternary; rondo, sonata or sonata
   rondo). Checks: which phrase is in rondo character (ear); which time
   signature fits; the third movement's form; the first movement's form.
6. **Review** (after 25.6): a terminative passage by ear, development against
   transition, the exposition's order (the book's "fill in the diagram"), the
   secondary theme's key in {E} minor (pad, {G}), sonatina form, the
   seven-part rondo, B's key in a {G} major rondo (pad, {D}), rondo character.

## Left out, and why

- **Every score excerpt and recording** (Mozart K.333, K.284, K.551, K.545,
  K.525; Haydn Hob. XVI:35; Beethoven Op.13 and Op.27 No.2; the rondo-character
  playlist; the practice exercises' recordings): images or videos without alt
  text. Each idea is shown with a short passage of our own (a primary theme in
  {C}, a secondary theme in {G} and again in {C}, a transition to V of {G}, a
  sequenced fragment, a V pedal, an I-V close, tiny rondos). Public-domain works
  are named, never quoted.
- **The sonata and rondo diagrams** (images) become text and, for the rondo,
  a staff of key chords with stacked labels.
- **The Grove, Green and Rosen quotations** are paraphrased in a sentence or
  two; Rosen is named for the sonata principle.
- **The Nutcracker timing table** (25.1.2) is left out; the overture is only
  named as an example of sonatina form.
- **Multiple secondary themes, new material in the development and missing
  closing themes** (25.1.1) are touched only briefly (the closing theme is
  "sometimes", the development "sometimes brings new material").
- **25.3's walk through the Pathétique** (transition, retransitions, the
  abbreviated refrain, the coda) is reduced to its key plan.
- **Exercises 1-3** (aural identification of real excerpts, filling in diagrams
  for K.545 and K.525) become checks on our own passages and on the
  exposition's order; the homework and test downloads are left out.

## Framework gaps hit

- A play block is one stream at one tempo with one sampled piano: no
  orchestral colour, no dynamics, and a "four movements in miniature" example
  can only suggest tempo through note values.
- Staccato dots cannot be drawn; staccato is played (short notes and rests).
- No way to draw a form diagram (boxes on a timeline); diagrams are text or a
  staff of chords with labels.
